\begin{abstract}
Workplace electric vehicle (EV) charging offers large technical flexibility, but how much of it is usable by the grid depends on what controllers know and what tariffs reward. We replay 13,623 open ACN-Data sessions from two California workplaces (July--December 2019) under a receding-horizon linear program that prices only increments above the month-to-date peak, with exact robust and PV-aware variants. Peak-state control roughly halves the site peak (296 to 140\,kW; 105 to 52\,kW), but acting on driver declarations leaves one vehicle in eight short of its energy. Robustness calibrated to empirical declaration errors cannot close this gap: declared energy is biased upwards and a third of drivers leave early. Re-using capacity already billed in the month restores service above 99\% without a new peak, confirmed on held-out 2021 sessions. The controllers turn this into grid-usable flexibility. They keep a transformer rated at half the uncontrolled peak within its rating while serving at least 99\% of energy, whereas uncontrolled charging overloads it twofold. With behind-the-meter PV, PV-aware control lowers the import peak to about a quarter of its uncontrolled value where arrivals precede solar output. A tariff sweep and stylised archetypes show that the presence of a demand charge, not its level or the energy-price structure, decides whether peak reduction is delivered, while evening-peak or flat energy prices remove the conflict with midday charging. Realisable EV flexibility therefore depends on information quality and tariff design, not only on parking duration and connected capacity.
\end{abstract}
